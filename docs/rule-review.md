# Adult singles: source review

This review covers the 16 July 2026 Adult announcement for Vernon 2026 and Oberstdorf 2027 and the sources listed in [rule-sources.md](rule-sources.md). The ISU Adult page lists the announcement on 17 August; the linked PDF itself states 16 July. The application does not yet validate programs or calculate scores.

## Findings

- **Scope and precedence.** The announcement applies to the two named events and incorporates the Sports Rules and pertinent Communications. When it conflicts with a regulation or Communication, the announcement prevails unless the Communication has special Adult comments (announcement pp. 2 and 4).
- **Euler.** Bronze allows one combination of two listed jumps and explicitly prohibits Euler in that combination (p. 11). Silver, Gold and Masters allow two combinations or a combination without Euler plus a sequence. Euler between listed jumps does not count as a listed jump, is limited to once per program, and cannot be followed by a change of foot before the next jump (pp. 8-10). The general handbook p. 33 describes the calls for a separate combination with Euler and a sequence. It also states the outcome when Euler appears twice. The exact consequence of a Bronze violation is not stated in the Adult announcement and cannot be assumed from those examples.
- **Waltz jump.** The Adult announcement says Bronze ignores it, while Silver and Gold call it as a downgraded single Axel (pp. 9-11). Communication 2786 p. 3 assigns `1A<<` a base value of zero. The handbook p. 28 also says an intended Waltz jump used only as preparation for another jump is not called. Its effect on element and repetition counts depends on the circumstances.
- **Calls and notation.** Communication 2786 p. 14 lists `q`, `<`, `<<`, `!`, `e` and `V` for the applicable element types; its `b` bonus is for Advanced Novice, outside this scope. The handbook pp. 2, 6, 25 and 30-33 covers `*`, `+REP`, `+COMBO` and `+SEQ`. Q&A 3, question 2, shows that individual jumps in a sequence can retain both their landing call and `*`. A fall and the second-half `x` are separate protocol information. This inventory covers these sources, not an assurance that future amendments introduce no other notation.
- **Second-half factor.** Sports Rule 353.1(h)(iii) requires a 1.1 base-value factor for the last three eligible free-skating jump elements started in the second half, without multiplying GOE. It specifies rounding and interruption cases. The Adult announcement incorporates the Sports Rules and has no explicit exception for the factor. Applying that general rule is the source-based default, subject to a specific event or Adult exception if one exists. No such exception was identified in the sources checked. Exact calculation also requires the start time and the interruption facts.
- **Bronze example.** `1Lz`, `1S+1T`, and a non-flying sit spin with three revolutions on each foot fit the stated individual Bronze provisions. Spiral and spread eagle are two different movements of the type listed for a choreographic sequence (p. 11). The example does not establish a complete legal or recognized program: execution details, spin feature recognition and other program-wide conditions still require evaluation.

## Planning and unknown information

An unfinished program may contain a selected jump whose rotation count has not yet been chosen, or a spin whose planned features have not yet been entered. Those values remain unspecified so the tool can retain the draft and identify the information needed for a complete check. The tool must not infer a jump, a call or a level from a missing field. An explicitly empty list of feature attempts means the skater has entered none; an unspecified list does not make that claim. Whether a user interface needs to expose this distinction is a product decision, not an ISU requirement.

A human domain review remains an explicit acceptance criterion of issue #1. The findings above are a source review, not a record of independent human approval.
