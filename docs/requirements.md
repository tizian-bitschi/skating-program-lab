# Requirements

## Purpose and scope

Skaters and coaches need to plan and compare technical programs. The MVP targets singles Free Skating at Bronze, Silver, Gold and Masters for the 2026/27 ISU-supported International Adult competitions named in the [sources](rule-sources.md). This is a planning aid, not an official panel or judging system. Rules and scoring tables are explicitly versioned; an unknown version must not be treated as compliant or scored by a fallback version.

Artistic skating, pairs, dance, national variations, other seasons and categories, PCS, accounts, persistence, UI and HTTP API are outside the MVP. Later work may add versions without overwriting old results. Full Technical Panel inference, individual judges' panel simulation, automatic detailed spin-level recognition and expected-TES optimization are later work.

## Four distinct stages

1. **Plan:** an ordered program of intended elements and their intended positions, features and transitions. The validator checks local requirements and program-wide counts and dependencies against a selected ruleset.
2. **Execution:** observations of what was actually done, including movements, rotations, transitions and falls. Missing observations remain unknown; execution may differ from the plan.
3. **Recognition:** the panel-recognized element code, level, calls and any no-value or ignored outcome. In the MVP these are supplied by the user, not inferred from video or complete motion descriptions. The system checks supported input consistency and must not pretend to replace a panel decision.
4. **Scoring:** the recognized code and calls select base values and GOE treatment; element scores sum to TES. Separately reported program deductions include falls and time violations. A planned violation, a non-recognized element, a reduced base value, a zero value and a deduction are distinct outcomes.

Results must distinguish plan violations, recognition or input inconsistencies, scoring consequences and unknown or unsupported facts. Each finding identifies affected element(s), stable rule identifier, explanation, source and location. A single `is_valid` flag is insufficient; missing or unsupported information cannot produce a complete compliance claim. Where an official consequence or interpretation is unresolved, return unknown rather than inventing a result.

## Category rules for the initial ruleset

The following is a compact scope, not a substitute for [the announcement](rule-sources.md) pp. 8–11. Limits concern the stated element type, not every movement in a program.

| Category | Jump elements and combinations | Spins and sequence | Fall deduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | At most 4; single jumps excluding Axel; at most one two-listed-jump combination, no Euler. | At most 2 different-abbreviation spins, both in one position, no flying entry; spin level cap 1; at most one choreographic sequence. | 0.5 per fall outside elements; none separately for a fall in an element. |
| Silver | At most 4; singles including 1A; no doubles or triples. | At most 2 different-abbreviation spins, one a non-flying one-position spin; level cap 2; at most one choreographic sequence. | As Bronze. |
| Gold | At most 5; singles and 2T, 2S, 2Lo; no 2F, 2Lz, 2A or triples. | At most 3 different-abbreviation spins: a non-flying combination with change of foot, a flying spin and a choreographic spin; regular spin cap 3, step cap 2; at most one step sequence. | 1.0 per fall. |
| Masters | At most 5; singles and doubles; an Axel type is required; no triples or quadruples. | At most 3 different-abbreviation spins: a non-flying combination, a flying spin without change of foot and a choreographic spin; level cap 4; at most one step sequence. | 1.0 per fall. |

The required durations are Bronze 1:40, Silver 2:00, Gold 2:50 and Masters 3:00, each with a ±10 second tolerance (Adult pp. 8–11). A time outside the permitted range incurs 1.0 per started five seconds, subject to the other timing and incomplete-program rules (Adult p. 6; Sports Rules 353 and 502).

All four categories limit a listed jump to two occurrences. Silver, Gold and Masters permit up to two combinations or one combination and one sequence; one can contain up to three listed jumps and the other up to two. The second and/or third jump of a sequence is an Axel type with the specified direct step. The announcement allows an Euler between listed jumps once per program and does not count it as a listed jump. When a combination and a sequence are performed, the combination must not use an Euler. After Euler no change of foot is allowed before the next jump. Bronze explicitly forbids Euler in its combination (Adult pp. 8–11). The general handbook gives consequences for separate Euler combinations and sequences; check Adult applicability before using those consequences. The validator counts jumps across the entire program, including components of combinations, and distinguishes combinations from sequences. It does not count movements within a choreographic sequence as independent elements without applying the applicable recognition rules.

The Adult announcement ignores a Waltz jump in Bronze; in Silver and Gold it is called as a downgraded single Axel (`1A<<`), which has zero BV in the Scale of Values. An ignored movement is distinct from a recognized zero-value element (Adult pp. 9–11). Its exact effect on element counting and repetitions must be checked against the applicable recognition rules, not assumed.

For regular spins the Adult minimum total revolutions without/with change of foot are Bronze 3/6, Silver 4/6, Gold 4/8 and Masters 5/8; Gold and Masters state these for the combination and flying spins, not the choreographic spin. No basic position with two revolutions yields no value. A spin with change of foot requires three revolutions on each foot; otherwise it is marked `V` (Adult pp. 8–11). Other recognition and minimum-revolution consequences depend on the precise case and rules; insufficient structured evidence yields unknown or the supplied panel outcome, not an invented call.

Regular spin recognition and level depend on positions, revolutions, change of foot and eligible features. Some features count only once across the ordered program: Communication 2788 p. 2 distinguishes first attempt for features 2–9 and 11–15 from first successful execution for feature 10. Choreographic spins have separate confirmation and fixed-base-value/GOE treatment; they do not receive an ordinary spin level. Q&A 1 requires the three consecutive revolutions on the same foot, on one or two blades. Choreographic and step sequences have different recognition, level and scoring rules. In Gold, a step sequence requires at least one skating movement such as a spiral, spread eagle or Ina Bauer; without it no level is given, and features 3, 4 and 5 are not awarded (Adult p. 9). MVP accepts recognized outcomes and supplied feature evidence; it checks supported dependencies and reports unknown when evidence or a rule is missing. Automatic derivation of all levels from detailed motions is later work.

## Scoring contract

The user supplies the recognized code and level, calls, final **single hypothetical judge's integer GOE** from -5 to +5, fall location, program duration and selected rule/table version. The user can provide observed execution separately. This is a scenario calculation, not an actual panel result; panel GOE is a trimmed mean of judges' numerical GOE values and is outside the MVP. Inputs are checked for supported codes, categories, mutually exclusive rotation calls (`q`, `<`, `<<`), edge calls (`!`, `e`), supported combinations and applicable GOE restrictions. An edge call on an inapplicable jump or contradictory marks yields an error; missing or undecidable evidence is unknown. An error must not silently change supplied calls or GOE. For a fall in an element, Communication 2788 pp. 14 and 16 gives a -5 reduction and a maximum starting GOE of +2; a supplied final single-judge GOE above -3 is inconsistent. Other aspects can lower the final GOE; a fall does not automatically make it -5.

For supported inputs, use the recognized element and call-specific entry in the selected Scale of Values, apply the corresponding GOE table value and prescribed rounding, and display base value, adjustments, GOE points and element score. `q` retains base value, `<` selects the under-rotated value, `<<` the downgraded value; `!` retains base value and `e` selects the applicable edge value. Their GOE effects are assessed separately; calls alone do not determine a final GOE. For combinations use the relevant table and reference-jump rule rather than a blanket percentage formula. The 1.1 second-half factor requires the jump-element start time and applicable interruption facts; see [sources](rule-sources.md). Apply the general rule as the source-based default and check for any event-specific Adult exception. Missing timing or an unresolved applicable exception makes the affected result incomplete. Sum element scores to TES. Show fall and time deductions separately; do not subtract them from TES. A later total-segment score requires separately calculated PCS and deductions. If an exact rule, rounding step or table is not supported, report the calculation as incomplete.

## Optimization contract

The first objective is maximum total base value in a finite, explicitly supplied candidate domain. Each request and result records ruleset and table version, ordered element variants (including jump types, rotations, combinations, sequences and transitions), possible levels, spin positions and features, relevant jump timing, assumptions, user exclusions, equivalence definition and enumeration limits. The domain contains only modeled variants; it is not a claim to contain every physically possible program. Individual ability or exclusions narrow that domain separately from official eligibility. The selected candidates must satisfy the implemented official rules and personal constraints, including program-wide feature use and ordering. Unresolved rules prevent a claim of full official compliance or an official maximum; they must not be silently ignored.

Report separately: proven optimum within the stated model; feasible incumbent with optimality unproven; proven infeasibility; interrupted or incomplete search; and equal optimal solutions with enumeration completeness separately proven or unknown. An interruption is neither proof of optimality nor infeasibility. Revalidate every candidate against the same rule definitions and independently recompute its base value. A solver's proof applies only to its stated candidate space and implemented constraints. Compare solver candidates later on a small enumerated instance, without a solver decision now.

## Acceptance cases

These are future behavior, not implemented tests. Assumptions are explicit; other findings may coexist. Source locations refer to [rule-sources.md](rule-sources.md). Human domain review is required before asserting complete correctness.

1. **Bronze boundaries:** 1A produces a prohibited-jump finding; four otherwise eligible jump elements do not trigger the count limit, five do. A two-listed-jump combination with Euler triggers the Euler prohibition (Adult p. 11). Two occurrences of 1Lz do not trigger repetition; a third anywhere in solo or combination does. Two jump combinations exceed the Bronze combination limit.
2. **Other categories:** Silver 2T is prohibited; Gold 2Lo is permitted by the jump-type rule but 2F and 2Lz are prohibited; Masters with no Axel type reports the missing requirement. In Silver, Gold or Masters a third combination violates the two-combination limit. These findings do not alone establish program validity (Adult pp. 8–10).
3. **Features and recognition:** Given two ordered regular spins attempting the same once-per-program feature, only its first attempt can earn that feature; feature 10 instead counts at first successful execution (2788 p. 2, subject to category caps). A recognized choreographic spin has fixed base value and no regular spin level; a recognized CSSp1 uses its level-specific value. Three consecutive revolutions split by a change of foot do not satisfy Q&A 1's same-foot requirement. Panel fallback identification remains a separate supplied decision.
4. **Table values:** Using the 2 June 2786 update, a recognized solo 1Lz started in the first half at final GOE +1 has BV 0.60, GOE +0.06 and element score 0.66; 1Lzq retains BV 0.60, 1Lz< has 0.48, 1Lz<< has 0.00, 1Lz! has 0.60, 1Lze has 0.48 and 1Lze< has 0.36 (p. 2). These are unfactored table BVs. Their error-related GOE reductions are not inferred from BV. A recognized ChSp1 with GOE +1 has BV 3.50, GOE +0.50 and score 4.00; CSSp1 with +1 has BV 2.30, GOE +0.23 and score 2.53 (pp. 8 and 14). No fall is assumed in these examples.
5. **Falls and time:** In Bronze, a fall inside a recognized element gives no separate fall deduction; with a supplied final single-judge GOE of -3 the applicable table value is used, while +1 is inconsistent with the fall's -5 reduction and starting cap. An outside fall adds 0.50 to separate deductions. A Bronze duration of 1:50 is within tolerance; 1:51 incurs a 1.00 time deduction, assuming no other time-related consequence (Adult p. 11; Sports Rules 353.1(n), 502; 2788 pp. 14, 16). TES remains the element-score sum.
6. **Optimizer and uncertainty:** Within an explicitly complete Masters candidate domain obeying the Axel requirement, excluding all Axel types yields proven infeasibility only if the search proves it. On a small independently enumerated domain, returned optima match all distinct maxima when enumeration is proven complete. A time limit with a feasible incumbent reports unproven optimality; without one it reports incomplete, not infeasible. Unknown rules or unsupported input cannot yield a full compliance or exact-score claim.
7. **Versioning:** Identical input and explicit versions yield identical results. Unknown versions are rejected; adding a season leaves earlier fixtures and source references unchanged.
8. **Gold step sequence:** With otherwise sufficient evidence, absence of the required skating movement yields no level. Features 3, 4 and 5 contribute no level credit; basic-level requirements are assessed independently (Adult p. 9).
9. **Waltz jump and spins:** In Bronze, a Waltz jump is ignored, not automatically reported as a prohibited planned 1A. In Silver and Gold, an executed Waltz jump is called `1A<<` with BV 0.00. For an otherwise recognized Bronze change-foot spin with two revolutions on one foot and at least three on the other, the stated per-foot condition yields `V`; a spin with no basic position held for two revolutions has no value. These are distinct recognition outcomes and do not infer an entire level (Adult pp. 9–11, 2786 p. 3).
10. **Silver duration:** 2:10 remains within tolerance; 2:11 produces a 1.00 separate time deduction, assuming no additional timing consequence. A jump eligible for the second-half factor without timing data or with an unresolved applicable exception must produce an incomplete affected result, not a silently unadjusted exact total.

## Quality requirements

Correctness and source traceability take priority over convenience and speed. Use deterministic exact decimal or integer arithmetic and source-defined rounding. Version rules and tables with precise citations; unresolved questions block affected compliance or exact-score claims. Pure domain functions support independent tests and eventual native and WebAssembly use. Keep modules and dependencies small. No performance target is asserted before a representative workload exists.

## Current model limitations

The library stores planned jump type and intended rotations independently; a missing value is unknown, not a legal or illegal jump. It does not yet validate category limits or calculate scores. A choreographic sequence can record planned movements, but free text does not establish recognition. Program metadata retains the selected scoring table and source references; compatibility and source contents are not validated.

Supplied recognition can record marks on a whole element or an ordered component and place +COMBO/+SEQ between components. The relevant notation includes q, <, <<, !, e, V, *, +REP, +COMBO and +SEQ (2786 p. 14; Technical Panel Handbook pp. 2, 6, 25, 30–33; Q&A 3 Q2). B and 1–4 are levels, F denotes a fall, and x denotes second-half credit on protocols; these are distinct from rotation and edge calls. x can be recorded as supplied; the calculation must still apply the event rules. RecognitionDetails stores ordered unmarked component codes, a separate level and calls as its canonical representation. ComponentCode rejects marked or unsupported codes. Its constructor rejects missing components, invalid targets, repeated calls, conflicting rotation or edge calls, and a level on multiple components, jumps, Euler or choreographic elements. Unknown calls remain distinct from an explicitly empty list; notation is generated only when calls are known. Component codes are limited to the listed jump names, Euler and the explicitly supported spin and sequence abbreviations; unknown codes require an explicit error. The constructor does not establish rule legality, event applicability or whether a call is applicable to a particular element. Future scoring must validate those conditions. The second-half factor requires timing, interruption facts and a check for applicable event-specific exceptions.
